# Brand craft

> The craft of a logo and the identity around it. Read after `index.md`, which says how.

## The job
A logo and the system around it. The mark is one part of the delivery: the client leaves with a way
of looking, so that a page with no logo on it still reads as theirs.

## Positioning questions
Answer each in one line, from the brief, before the first shape.

- What does only this client have: a place, a trade, a history, a way of talking?
- Where does the mark live smallest: a favicon, a stamp, a line on a receipt?
- Who must it not be mistaken for: the direct rivals, and any name that sounds close?

## What great looks like
- The swap test: picture a rival's name on it and something feels wrong. The idea belongs to this
  client, and nobody else in the category could claim it.
- One idea that survives 16 px and one colour: at favicon size, in black, it is still the same
  mark and not a blur of its details.
- A wordmark considered before a symbol: the name is set and shown first, and a symbol appears only
  where it says what the name alone cannot.
- A system, not a picture: type, colour and a pattern of use are defined well enough that an
  application with the logo covered still reads as this client.
- An original shape, checked against the category's marks: the caption says in words what was ruled
  out, with no rival's name or logo on the page.

## Refuse
- A letter in a rounded square: the default icon, true of every company with that initial.
- A swoosh, an orbit, a generic spark: motion, reach and intelligence, claimed for anyone.
- A gradient standing in for an idea: set it in one colour and nothing is left.
- A mascot by default: a character comes from the client's own story or not at all.
- Any shape near a real mark: drop it, even when it is the best one on the board.

## Deliverables
- The logo as inline SVG: primary, one-colour and reversed, each at 16, 32 and 64 px, with its clear
  space drawn.
- A type pair, set in real sentences, with what each face is for.
- A palette with roles: each colour named for its job, ground, ink or accent, not for its hue.
- The voice in three lines.
- Three applications, taken from where the mark really lives.

## Optional skills
- `brandkit`: its core-principle questions and strategy list, which tie the mark to a metaphor
  before any shape. Its symbol table suggests what `## Refuse` bans: leave it.
- `high-end-visual-design`: its vibe and texture archetypes, as material for the palette and the
  type pair, without the glow.
- `minimalist-ui`: colour as a scarce resource, and the contrast between its serif and its sans.
- `industrial-brutalist-ui`: extreme type contrast and a visible grid, for a raw or technical
  client.
- `frontend-design`: the traits generated design clusters around: hold the palette and the pair
  against them.
- `ui-ux-pro-max`: the typography and colour row of its priority table, for contrast and roles.
- `impeccable`: its craft floor and detector, as `index.md` says.
