# Website craft

> The craft of public pages and web products. Read after `index.md`, which says how.

## The job
Public pages and web products. A page makes one client's case to a stranger who owes it nothing,
and the first screen decides whether they stay.

## Positioning questions
Answer each in one line, from the brief, before the first section.

- What must a stranger grasp in five seconds?
- What can only this client show: its work, its people, a process, a figure of its own?
- What must the page make them do: which one action?

## What great looks like
- One idea from the client's own material carries the first screen: without the logo, it could
  still belong to nobody else in the category.
- Type set large, with a real scale: the headline works as a shape on the screen, and each step down
  is a visible decision.
- A whole field of colour rather than a timid accent: at least one screen is owned by a colour, edge
  to edge.
- Varied rhythm, one section someone would remember: heights and densities change down the page,
  and you can point at the section a visitor would describe to a friend.
- Real content at full length: the longest true title and the whole paragraph, not a short stand-in
  that flatters the layout.
- Every width composed, not stacked by default: the narrowest and the widest frames each show a
  decision made for that width, in order, size or crop.

## Refuse
- A centred headline over three lines and a row of icon cards: the page every generator opens with.
- A soft purple gradient: a fingerprint of generated design, whatever the subject.
- A small label above every heading: a label stays only where it tells the reader something.
- A bento grid with nothing to arrange: cells filled because the grid has cells.
- A wall of invented logos or figures: proof nobody can stand behind. Real, or absent.
- Ornament removed and nothing added: a bare page is not yet a point of view.

## Deliverables
- The first screen and one full scroll at each width of `design.widths`.
- The nav and the footer.
- The states: hover, focus, and each form's error and success.
- All of it in light and dark.

## Optional skills
- `design-taste-frontend`: the signals and the one-line Design Read of its section 0, then its
  anti-default list. Its stack and package rules stay out.
- `high-end-visual-design`: macro whitespace and spatial rhythm. Its eyebrow tags, purple glow and
  bento are what `## Refuse` bans.
- `gpt-taste`: the AIDA order of a page and a hero held to three lines at most. Its random draw and
  its placeholder images stay out.
- `minimalist-ui`: typographic contrast and its bans on stock copy, for a quiet direction. Its ban
  on a coloured section stays out: this craft wants the field.
- `industrial-brutalist-ui`: extreme scale contrast and visible compartments, for a raw or
  technical direction.
- `redesign-existing-projects`: the audit's lists of tells: hold a draft against them, and on a live
  site keep what works.
- `emil-design-eng`: the animation decision framework: whether to animate, why, which easing, how
  fast.
- `apple-design`: response on press and motion that can be interrupted, for anything dragged or
  swiped.
- `frontend-design`: opening on the most characteristic thing in the subject's world, and the traits
  generated design clusters around.
- `ui-ux-pro-max`: its priority table, for the accessibility, touch and layout checks a style must
  not break.
- `imagegen-frontend-web`: the hero compositions it offers in place of left text and right image,
  and its list of defaults to break.
- `image-to-code`: its list of defaults to break. Its image-first rule stays out.
- `impeccable`: its craft floor and detector, as `index.md` says.
